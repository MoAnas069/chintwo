02 / STUDIO & PHILOSOPHY

A Story in Ink, Atmosphere & Memory

Working between Kyoto washi craft and atmospheric chiaroscuro, Chintwo crafts evocative visual narratives investigating negative space, raw mineral pigments, and quiet moments of solitude.

CHAPTER 01

Origins & Japanese Washi Roots

My formative studio apprenticeship in the Echizen valley shaped everything I understand about surface tension. There, fourth-generation papermakers cook raw kozo (mulberry) bark in mountain spring water, beating fibers by hand on river stone until they surrender their rigidity.

When sumi ink touches handmade washi, it is not merely placed on top; the capillary pull of individual unbleached fibers draws the pigment inward, creating soft, feathery penumbras impossible to reproduce mechanically. This tactile alchemy remains the anchor of every composition.

"Paper is not an inert substrate; it is an active breathing collaborator in the image."
ARCHIVE • ECHIZEN PAPERS
STUDY • MA & NEGATIVE SPACE
CHAPTER 02

The Architecture of Silence

In western traditions, space is frequently treated as empty void waiting to be conquered. In Japanese aesthetics, the concept of Ma treats the space between objects as pregnant with meaning, sound, and temporal weight.

When designing narrative book covers and large canvases, my earliest gestures are subtractive: stripping away ornamental foliage, muting secondary light sources, and allowing unbroken stretches of deep charcoal and raw alabaster to command the emotional tone.

"A quiet picture does not demand attention; it invites the viewer to step into stillness."
CHAPTER 03

Pigment & Stone Alchemy

All colors in the atelier are ground by hand using an agate mortar and pestle. Crushed lapis lazuli from Badakhshan, burnt bone blacks, and soot collected from pine resin lanterns provide deep velvety textures that synthetic acrylics cannot match.

By suspending these pure mineral granules in traditional rabbit-skin sizing (nikawa), the finished surface captures changing ambient light throughout the day, shifting subtly from velvety graphite at dawn to glowing twilight hues at dusk.

"Earth, pine soot, and river stone carry geological patience into modern mark-making."
MATERIALS • NATURAL MINERAL PIGMENTS
LONDON ATELIER • ROCHAT PRESS
CHAPTER 04

The Living Studio Ritual

Operating from an airy converted warehouse in East London, the studio relies exclusively on diffuse north illumination. A vintage cast-iron Rochat monotype press sits alongside rows of raw paper racks, sumi grinding stones, and archival drying felts.

Each day starts with thirty minutes of ink grinding in silent concentration. It is not ritual for its own sake, but a deliberate slowing down of the mind to match the rhythm of printing, pulling, and contemplating.

"The studio is a sanctuary where hurried thoughts are distilled into enduring marks."
